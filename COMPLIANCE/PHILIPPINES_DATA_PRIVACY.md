## Overview of RA 10173

### The Data Privacy Act of 2012
Republic Act No. 10173, also known as the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (DPA), is the primary data protection law in the Philippines. It was enacted to protect the fundamental right to privacy of communication while ensuring the free flow of information.

### Key Definitions
- **Personal Information**: Any information from which the identity of an individual is apparent or can be reasonably ascertained.
- **Sensitive Personal Information**: Information about an individual's race, ethnicity, political opinions, religious/philosophical beliefs, health, sexual life, and government-issued IDs.
- **Personal Information Controller (PIC)**: Entity that controls the collection, holding, processing, or use of personal information.
- **Personal Information Processor (PIP)**: Entity that processes personal information on behalf of a PIC.
- **Data Subject**: An individual whose personal information is processed.

### Scope of Application
- Applies to any natural or juridical person processing personal data in the Philippines.
- Applies to entities outside the Philippines if they process personal data of Filipino citizens or residents.
- Covers both government and private sector entities.

### Governing Body
**National Privacy Commission (NPC)** — the independent regulatory body responsible for enforcing the DPA.

## NPC Registration Requirements

### Who Must Register
All Personal Information Controllers (PICs) and Personal Information Processors (PIPs) must register with the NPC if they:
1. Employ at least 250 persons.
2. Process sensitive personal information.
3. Process personal information of at least 1,000 data subjects.
4. Engage in regular processing of personal information as a core business activity.

### Registration Process

| Step | Description | Timeline |
|------|-------------|----------|
| 1 | Designate Data Protection Officer (DPO) | Before registration |
| 2 | Prepare DPO identity and contact details | Before registration |
| 3 | Complete online registration form via NPC portal | On registration |
| 4 | Submit supporting documents | With registration |
| 5 | Pay registration fee | Along with registration |
| 6 | Receive Certificate of Registration | Within 30 days |

### Required Documents for Registration
- Company registration documents (SEC, DTI, or CDA).
- DPO appointment letter.
- DPO qualifications and contact information.
- Description of personal data processing activities.
- Data processing systems overview.
- Organizational security measures.
- Physical security measures.
- Technical security measures.

### Annual Reporting
- Registered entities must submit annual compliance reports.
- Reports include updates to processing activities, security measures, and data breaches.
- Submission deadline: Every year on or before the anniversary of initial registration.

### Registration Exemptions
- Individuals processing personal data for personal or household purposes.
- Government entities (but must still comply with all other requirements).
- Small businesses with fewer than 250 employees and processing fewer than 1,000 data subjects' personal information — unless they process sensitive personal information.

## Data Breach Notification

### Definition of Personal Data Breach
A breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access to, personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed.

### Types of Breaches
1. **Confidentiality breach**: Unauthorized disclosure of or access to personal data.
2. **Integrity breach**: Unauthorized alteration of personal data.
3. **Availability breach**: Accidental or unauthorized loss of access to or destruction of personal data.

### Notification Requirements

#### Notification to NPC
- **Timeline**: Within 72 hours of knowledge of or reasonable belief that a breach has occurred.
- **Method**: Submit via NPC Data Breach Notification Portal.
- **Required Information**:
  - Nature of the breach.
  - Date/time of discovery and occurrence.
  - Types and approximate number of affected data subjects.
  - Types and approximate amount of affected personal data.
  - Potential consequences and adverse effects.
  - Security measures in place before the breach.
  - Remedial measures taken or proposed.
  - Contact information of DPO or responsible officer.

#### Notification to Affected Data Subjects
- **Trigger**: When the breach is likely to cause substantial harm or affect the data subject's rights.
- **Timeline**: Within 72 hours of knowledge of the breach.
- **Required Content**:
  - Description of the breach.
  - Types of personal data involved.
  - Measures taken to address the breach.
  - Recommendations for affected data subjects to protect themselves.
  - Contact information for inquiries.

#### Notification to Other Authorities
- Notify law enforcement if the breach involves criminal activity.
- Notify relevant government agencies if the breach affects public services.

### Breach Response Process
1. **Detection and assessment** (within 24 hours): Identify and assess the breach.
2. **Containment** (within 48 hours): Stop the breach and prevent further damage.
3. **Notification** (within 72 hours): Notify NPC and affected data subjects.
4. **Investigation**: Determine root cause and scope.
5. **Remediation**: Implement corrective measures.
6. **Documentation**: Complete breach report and post-incident review.
7. **Prevention**: Update security measures to prevent recurrence.

### Record Keeping
- Maintain records of all data breaches, including those not requiring notification.
- Records must include facts, effects, and remedial actions.
- Retention period: 5 years from the date of the breach.

### Penalties for Non-Notification
- Failure to notify the NPC: Fine of up to PHP 5 million.
- Failure to notify affected data subjects: Fine of up to PHP 2 million.
- Additional penalties may apply depending on severity.

## Consent Requirements

### General Consent Principles
- Consent must be **freely given**, **specific**, **informed**, and **unambiguous**.
- Consent must be obtained **before** processing personal data.
- Consent must be **documented** and **verifiable**.
- Data subjects have the right to **withdraw consent** at any time.

### Valid Consent Criteria
1. **Freely given**: No coercion, duress, or undue influence.
2. **Specific**: Consent for a particular purpose cannot be blanket consent.
3. **Informed**: Data subject must understand what they are consenting to.
4. **Unambiguous**: Clear affirmative action required (no pre-ticked boxes).

### Explicit Consent for Sensitive Personal Information
Processing of sensitive personal information requires **explicit consent**:
- Separate from general consent.
- Specific acknowledgement of the sensitive nature of the data.
- Explanation of why sensitive data is needed.
- Clear description of processing purposes.

### Consent Withdrawal
- Data subject may withdraw consent at any time.
- Withdrawal does not affect lawfulness of processing before withdrawal.
- Process for withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent.
- Withdrawal must be documented.

### Exceptions to Consent Requirement
Consent is not required in the following circumstances:
1. **Legal obligation**: Processing is required by law.
2. **Contractual necessity**: Processing is necessary for a contract with the data subject.
3. **Vital interests**: Processing is necessary to protect life or health.
4. **Public interest**: Processing is necessary for public function or public order.
5. **Legitimate interests**: Processing is necessary for legitimate interests of the PIC, provided it does not override data subject rights.

### Consent Documentation
- Consent records must be maintained.
- Records must include: when consent was given, what was disclosed, how consent was obtained.
- Retention: Duration of processing + 3 years.

## Data Subject Rights Under RA 10173

### Right to be Informed (Section 16a)
- Data subjects have the right to be informed whether personal data is being processed.
- Information must include purpose, scope, method, and recipients of processing.
- Disclosure must be in plain language.

### Right to Object (Section 16b)
- Data subjects may object to processing of their personal data.
- Processing must cease unless there is a compelling legitimate interest.
- Applies particularly to direct marketing and profiling.

### Right to Access (Section 16c)
- Data subjects have the right to access their personal data being processed.
- Request must be responded to within **15 working days**.
- PIC may charge a reasonable fee for additional copies.

### Access Request Contents
- Confirmation of whether data is being processed.
- Description of personal data held.
- Purpose of processing.
- Recipients or categories of recipients.
- Source of the data (if not collected directly).
- Information about automated decision-making.
- Period for which data will be stored.

### Right to Rectification (Section 16d)
- Data subjects may request correction of inaccurate or incomplete data.
- PIC must correct the data immediately upon discovery of inaccuracy.
- Disputed data must be marked as disputed until resolution.

### Right to Erasure or Blocking (Section 16e)
- Data subjects may request suspension, withdrawal, or blocking of processing.
- Grounds include:
  - Data is incomplete, outdated, false, or unlawfully obtained.
  - Processing is for unauthorized purposes.
  - Data subject withdraws consent.
- PIC must notify third parties processing the data.

### Right to Damages (Section 16f)
- Data subjects may claim damages for violations of the DPA.
- Applies to actual and moral damages.
- Burden of proof is on the PIC to show no violation occurred.

### Right to Data Portability (Section 18)
- Data subjects may receive their personal data in a commonly used format.
- Applies to processed data provided by the data subject.
- Right exists when processing is based on consent or contract.
- Must be electronic and structured format.

### Exercise of Rights
| Right | Request Method | Response Timeline | Cost |
|-------|----------------|-------------------|------|
| Be informed | Written/electronic | 15 working days | Free |
| Object | Written/electronic | 15 working days | Free |
| Access | Written/electronic | 15 working days | Free (first copy) |
| Rectification | Written/electronic | 15 working days | Free |
| Erasure/Blocking | Written/electronic | 15 working days | Free |
| Portability | Written/electronic | 30 working days | Free |
| Damages | Court filing | Per court rules | Per court |

## Cross-Border Transfer Rules

### General Principle
Personal information may be transferred outside the Philippines only if the receiving country ensures a level of protection comparable to the DPA.

### Criteria for Adequate Protection
The NPC evaluates the following factors:
1. Legal framework of the receiving country (data protection laws).
2. Regulatory enforcement mechanisms.
3. International commitments of the receiving country.
4. Binding corporate rules or contractual clauses.
5. Security measures implemented by the recipient.

### Permitted Transfer Mechanisms

1. **Contractual Clauses**
   - Model contractual clauses approved by the NPC.
   - Must include: purpose limitation, data security, breach notification, data subject rights, liability.
   - Must be signed by both parties.

2. **Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs)**
   - For intra-group transfers.
   - Must be approved by the NPC.
   - Must cover all group entities receiving the data.
   - Enforceable data protection policies.

3. **Consent**
   - Data subject explicitly consents to the transfer after being informed of the risks.
   - Must include: absence of adequate protection, specific risks, rights in the receiving country.
   - Consent must be specific to the transfer.

4. **Contractual Necessity**
   - Transfer is necessary for contract performance with the data subject.
   - Transfer is necessary for contract conclusion at the data subject's request.

5. **Legal Obligation**
   - Transfer is required by law or international agreement.

6. **Vital Interests**
   - Transfer is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject.

### Due Diligence Requirements
Before transferring data cross-border, the PIC must:
1. Assess the recipient's data protection practices.
2. Review the recipient's privacy policies and security measures.
3. Verify the recipient's compliance with applicable laws.
4. Document the due diligence process.
5. Maintain records of all cross-border transfers.

### Prohibited Transfers
- Transfers to countries or entities without adequate protection (unless an exception applies).
- Transfers that would violate other Philippine laws (e.g., bank secrecy, non-disclosure obligations).
- Transfers that would circumvent the DPA's protections.

### Notification Requirements
- Cross-border transfers must be disclosed to data subjects in the privacy notice.
- Significant transfers (bulk data, sensitive data) may require NPC notification.
- Annual report to NPC must include cross-border transfer details.

### Enforcement and Penalties
- Unauthorized cross-border transfer: Fine of up to PHP 5 million.
- Processing of personal data due to negligence: Imprisonment of 1-3 years.
- Processing of sensitive personal data due to negligence: Imprisonment of 3-6 years.
- Repeat violations: Maximum penalties.

## Penalties and Enforcement

### Criminal Penalties

| Violation | Imprisonment | Fine |
|-----------|-------------|------|
| Unauthorized processing of personal information | 1-3 years | PHP 500,000 - PHP 4,000,000 |
| Unauthorized processing of sensitive personal information | 3-6 years | PHP 1,000,000 - PHP 5,000,000 |
| Access due to negligence | 1-3 years | PHP 500,000 - PHP 4,000,000 |
| Improper disposal of personal data | 6 months - 2 years | PHP 100,000 - PHP 2,000,000 |
| Processing of personal data for unauthorized purposes | 1-3 years | PHP 500,000 - PHP 4,000,000 |
| Unauthorized access or intentional breach | 1-3 years | PHP 500,000 - PHP 4,000,000 |
| Concealment of security breaches | 1-5 years | PHP 500,000 - PHP 4,000,000 |

### Administrative Penalties
- Cease and desist orders.
- Temporary or permanent ban on processing.
- Revocation of registration.
- Penalty of up to PHP 5,000,000 for violations.

### Civil Liability
- Data subjects may claim actual and moral damages.
- Exemplary damages may be awarded in cases of gross negligence.
- PIC bears the burden of proving no violation occurred.
