## Overview of GDPR

### Regulation (EU) 2016/679
The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is the European Union's comprehensive data protection law, effective May 25, 2018. It governs the processing of personal data of individuals in the EU/EEA.

### Key Principles (Article 5)
1. **Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency**: Processing must be lawful, fair, and transparent to the data subject.
2. **Purpose limitation**: Data must be collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes.
3. **Data minimization**: Data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary.
4. **Accuracy**: Data must be accurate and kept up to date.
5. **Storage limitation**: Data must be kept no longer than necessary.
6. **Integrity and confidentiality**: Data must be processed securely.
7. **Accountability**: The controller is responsible for demonstrating compliance.

### Territorial Scope (Article 3)
- **Establishment criterion**: Processing in the context of an EU establishment.
- **Targeting criterion**: Processing of EU data subjects' data offering goods/services or monitoring behavior.
- Regardless of whether processing occurs within the EU.

### Key Definitions
- **Personal Data**: Any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person.
- **Processing**: Any operation performed on personal data (collection, storage, use, deletion, etc.).
- **Controller**: Entity determining the purposes and means of processing.
- **Processor**: Entity processing data on behalf of the controller.
- **Data Subject**: The individual whose personal data is processed.
- **Special Category Data**: Racial/ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs, health, sex life, genetic/biometric data.

## Lawful Basis for Processing

### Six Lawful Bases (Article 6)
Processing is lawful only if at least one of the following applies:

| Lawful Basis | Description | When to Use | Examples |
|-------------|-------------|-------------|----------|
| **Consent** (Art. 6(1)(a)) | Data subject has given unambiguous consent | When no other basis applies; marketing; cookies | Email marketing opt-in |
| **Contract** (Art. 6(1)(b)) | Processing necessary for contract performance | Account creation, service delivery | Payment processing, account management |
| **Legal Obligation** (Art. 6(1)(c)) | Processing required by law | Tax, employment law, regulatory compliance | Tax record keeping |
| **Vital Interests** (Art. 6(1)(d)) | Processing necessary to protect someone's life | Emergency medical situations | Hospital sharing patient data in emergency |
| **Public Task** (Art. 6(1)(e)) | Processing necessary for public interest or official authority | Government functions | Census data collection |
| **Legitimate Interests** (Art. 6(1)(f)) | Processing necessary for legitimate interests of controller or third party | When other bases don't apply and interests don't override data subject rights | Fraud prevention, network security |

### Special Category Data (Article 9)
Processing special category data requires:
1. Explicit consent, OR
2. Employment/social security law, OR
3. Vital interests (data subject physically/legally incapable), OR
4. Legitimate activities of a foundation/association, OR
5. Data manifestly made public by data subject, OR
6. Legal claims, OR
7. Substantial public interest, OR
8. Health/social care purposes, OR
9. Public health reasons, OR
10. Archiving/research purposes.

### Legitimate Interest Assessment (LIA)
When relying on legitimate interests, document:
1. **Purpose test**: What is the legitimate interest?
2. **Necessity test**: Is processing necessary for that purpose?
3. **Balancing test**: Do the data subject's interests override the legitimate interest?

### Processing for Criminal Convictions (Article 10)
- Can only be processed under official authority or with appropriate safeguards.
- Requires specific legal basis under Member State law.

## Data Subject Rights

### Right to be Informed (Articles 13-14)
Information to provide when collecting data:
- Controller identity and contact details.
- DPO contact details.
- Purpose and lawful basis for processing.
- Legitimate interests (if relying on Art. 6(1)(f)).
- Recipients or categories of recipients.
- Intention to transfer data internationally and safeguards.
- Retention period or criteria.
- Right to access, rectify, erase, restrict, object, and portability.
- Right to withdraw consent.
- Right to lodge complaint with supervisory authority.
- Whether providing data is a statutory/contractual requirement.
- Existence of automated decision-making and logic involved.

**Timeline**: Provide at the time of data collection (Art. 13) or within 1 month if obtained from third party (Art. 14).

### Right of Access (Article 15)
Data subjects have the right to:
- Confirmation of whether data is being processed.
- Access to their personal data.
- Information about processing purposes, categories, recipients, retention.
- Copy of personal data (first copy free).

**Response timeline**: Within 1 month (extendable to 2-3 months for complex requests).

### Right to Rectification (Article 16)
- Data subject may request correction of inaccurate data.
- Controller must rectify without undue delay.
- Must notify recipients of rectification (Article 19).

### Right to Erasure (Right to be Forgotten) (Article 17)
Data subject may request erasure when:
1. Data is no longer necessary for original purpose.
2. Consent is withdrawn and no other lawful basis exists.
3. Data subject objects and there are no overriding legitimate grounds.
4. Data was unlawfully processed.
5. Erasure is required by EU/Member State law.
6. Data was collected from a child for information society services.

**Exceptions**: Erasure not required for:
- Exercise of freedom of expression.
- Legal obligation.
- Public health purposes.
- Archiving/research purposes.
- Establishment/exercise/defense of legal claims.

### Right to Restrict Processing (Article 18)
Data subject may restrict processing when:
1. Accuracy of data is contested (pending verification).
2. Processing is unlawful and data subject opposes erasure.
3. Controller no longer needs data but data subject requires it for legal claims.
4. Data subject has objected to processing (pending verification).

Restricted data can only be stored; further processing requires consent or legal claims.

### Right to Data Portability (Article 20)
- Right to receive data in structured, commonly used, machine-readable format.
- Right to transmit data directly to another controller (where technically feasible).
- Applies only to: data provided by the data subject, processed by automated means, based on consent or contract.

### Right to Object (Article 21)
- **Direct marketing**: Unconditional right to object at any time.
- **Legitimate interests/public task**: Right to object unless controller demonstrates compelling legitimate grounds.
- **Scientific/historical research**: Right to object unless processing is necessary for public interest.

### Automated Individual Decision-Making (Article 22)
Data subjects have the right:
- Not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing that produce legal effects or similarly significant effects.
- To obtain human intervention.
- To express their point of view.
- To contest the decision.

**Exceptions**: Necessary for contract, authorized by law, or based on explicit consent.

### Exercise of Rights Summary
| Right | Response Timeline | Exceptions | Fee |
|-------|-------------------|------------|-----|
| Informed | At collection / 1 month | None | Free |
| Access | 1 month (extendable) | Manifestly unfounded or excessive | Free (first copy) |
| Rectification | Without undue delay | None | Free |
| Erasure | Without undue delay | See exceptions above | Free |
| Restrict | Without undue delay | None | Free |
| Portability | 1 month (extendable) | Not technically feasible | Free |
| Object | Without undue delay | Compelling legitimate grounds | Free |

## Consent Management

### Valid Consent Requirements (Article 7)
Consent must be:
1. **Freely given**: Genuine choice; no imbalance of power; not conditional on service performance.
2. **Specific**: Tied to a particular purpose; granular consent for different purposes.
3. **Informed**: Clear explanation of what data is collected, why, and by whom.
4. **Unambiguous**: Clear affirmative action (no silence, pre-ticked boxes, or inactivity).

### Explicit Consent (Article 9)
Required for special category data. Must include:
- Clear statement of agreement.
- Separate from general consent.
- Specific to the special category processing.

### Consent Withdrawal (Article 7(3))
- Data subject may withdraw consent at any time.
- Withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent.
- Withdrawal does not affect lawfulness of processing before withdrawal.
- Data subject must be informed of this right before giving consent.

### Consent for Children (Article 8)
- For information society services: minimum age of digital consent is 16 (Member States may lower to 13).
- Consent must be given or authorized by the holder of parental responsibility.
- Controller must make reasonable efforts to verify age and parental consent.

### Consent Records
Document for each consent:
- Who gave consent.
- When consent was given.
- What was disclosed at the time of consent.
- How consent was obtained.
- Whether consent was withdrawn and when.

### Consent Management System Requirements
- Granular consent options per processing purpose.
- Clear affirmative action mechanism (e.g., checkbox, button).
- Withdrawal mechanism equally accessible.
- Audit trail of all consent transactions.
- Consent renewal after significant changes.
- Cookie consent compliant with ePrivacy Directive.

### Processing Without Consent
If relying on another lawful basis, remember:
- Consent is not required.
- Right to object may still apply.
- Transparency obligations still apply.
- Record-keeping obligations still apply.

## DPO Information

### When a DPO is Required (Article 37)
Mandatory appointment of a Data Protection Officer if:
1. Processing is carried out by a public authority or body.
2. Core activities require regular and systematic monitoring of data subjects on a large scale.
3. Core activities consist of processing special category data or criminal conviction data on a large scale.

### DPO Role and Responsibilities
- **Monitor compliance**: Advise on and monitor GDPR compliance.
- **Advise**: Conduct Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs).
- **Cooperate**: Liaise with supervisory authority.
- **Point of contact**: Serve as contact for data subjects and supervisory authority.
- **Training**: Deliver data protection training.
- **Audits**: Conduct internal audits.

### DPO Position Requirements
- **Expertise**: Expert knowledge of data protection law and practices.
- **Independence**: Must perform duties independently; no conflicts of interest.
- **Reporting**: Report directly to the highest management level.
- **Resources**: Must have sufficient resources to perform duties.
- **Protection**: Must not be penalized for performing DPO duties.

### DPO Contact Information
Must be published:
- **Email**: dpo@company.com
- **Address**: [Company physical address]
- **Phone**: [DPO direct line]
- **Supervisory authority**: [Name and contact of lead supervisory authority]

### DPO Records
- DPO appointment letter.
- DPO qualifications and experience.
- DPO training records.
- DPO activity reports.
- DPO communication log with supervisory authority.

## Cross-Border Transfers

### General Restriction (Article 44)
Personal data may only be transferred to third countries or international organizations if the conditions in Articles 45-49 are met. This ensures the level of protection is essentially equivalent to GDPR.

### Adequacy Decisions (Article 45)
The European Commission has determined that the following countries provide adequate protection:
- Andorra, Argentina, Canada (commercial organizations), Faroe Islands, Guernsey, Israel, Isle of Man, Japan, Jersey, New Zealand, Republic of Korea, San Marino, South Korea, Switzerland, United Kingdom (with conditions), Uruguay, United States (under Data Privacy Framework).

### Appropriate Safeguards (Article 46)
If no adequacy decision exists, transfers may proceed with appropriate safeguards:

1. **Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs)**:
   - EU Commission-approved SCCs (2021 version).
   - Covers controller-to-controller and controller-to-processor transfers.
   - Must include Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA).
   - Supplementary measures may be required.

2. **Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs)**:
   - Approved by supervisory authority.
   - Covers intra-group transfers.
   - Must include enforceable data protection policies.
   - Requires annual reporting.

3. **Approved Codes of Conduct** (Article 40).
4. **Approved Certification Mechanisms** (Article 42).
5. **Contractual clauses authorized by supervisory authority**.

### Derogations for Specific Situations (Article 49)
When no adequate decision or safeguards exist, transfers may proceed only if:
1. **Explicit consent**: Data subject explicitly consents after being informed of the risks.
2. **Contract necessity**: Transfer necessary for contract performance or on data subject's request.
3. **Important public interest**: Transfer necessary for important public interest grounds.
4. **Legal claims**: Transfer necessary for establishment, exercise, or defense of legal claims.
5. **Vital interests**: Transfer necessary to protect vital interests of data subject or others.
6. **Public register**: Transfer from a public register.
7. **Legitimate interests**: (Only for limited transfers) Controller has compelling legitimate interest, no repetitive or large-scale transfers.

### Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA)
Required for transfers based on SCCs. Must assess:
1. The transfer details (types of data, purposes, parties).
2. The legal framework of the recipient country.
3. The specific safeguards implemented.
4. Supplementary measures to ensure equivalent protection.
5. Risk assessment and mitigation.

### Supplemental Measures
Examples of measures to address third-country risks:
- Technical: Encryption, pseudonymization, data masking.
- Organizational: Access controls, training, audits.
- Contractual: Enhanced contractual provisions, audit rights.
- Transparency: Transparency reports, data access logs.

### Schrems II Compliance
Following the Schrems II ruling (CJEU, July 2020):
- SCCs alone may not be sufficient.
- Must assess third country surveillance laws.
- Must implement supplementary measures where needed.
- Must suspend transfers if equivalent protection cannot be ensured.

## Breach Notification

### Definition of Personal Data Breach (Article 4(12))
A breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access to, personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed.

### Notification to Supervisory Authority (Article 33)
- **Timeline**: Within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach.
- **Content required**:
  - Nature of the breach (categories and approximate number of data subjects and records).
  - DPO or contact point details.
  - Likely consequences of the breach.
  - Measures taken or proposed to address the breach and mitigate effects.
- **Delayed notification**: Must include reasons for delay with the notification.

### Notification to Data Subjects (Article 34)
- **Trigger**: When the breach is likely to result in a high risk to data subjects' rights and freedoms.
- **Timeline**: Without undue delay.
- **Content required**:
  - Description of the nature of the breach.
  - DPO or contact point details.
  - Likely consequences.
  - Measures taken or proposed to mitigate harm.
- **Exceptions** (no notification required):
  1. Data was encrypted or otherwise rendered unintelligible.
  2. Controller has taken subsequent measures that eliminate high risk.
  3. Disproportionate effort (public communication instead).

### Documentation Obligations (Article 33(5))
- Document all breaches, including those not requiring notification.
- Record: facts, effects, remedial actions taken.
- Enable supervisory authority to verify compliance.

### Breach Response Process
1. **Detection**: Identify potential breach.
2. **Containment**: Stop breach and preserve evidence.
3. **Assessment**: Determine scope, severity, and risk.
4. **Notification**: Notify supervisory authority (72 hours) and data subjects (if required).
5. **Investigation**: Root cause analysis.
6. **Remediation**: Implement corrective measures.
7. **Review**: Post-incident review and update security measures.

### Penalties for Non-Compliance
- Administrative fines up to EUR 10 million or 2% of global annual turnover (whichever is higher) — for breach notification failures.
- Administrative fines up to EUR 20 million or 4% of global annual turnover (whichever is higher) — for non-compliance with basic principles, consent, data subject rights, or transfers.

## Accountability and Governance

### Records of Processing Activities (Article 30)
Must maintain written records including:
- Controller/processor contact information.
- Purposes of processing.
- Categories of data subjects and personal data.
- Categories of recipients.
- International transfer details and safeguards.
- Retention periods.
- Technical and organizational security measures.

### Data Protection Impact Assessment (Article 35)
Required when processing is likely to result in high risk, including:
- Systematic and extensive profiling with significant effects.
- Large-scale processing of special category data.
- Systematic monitoring of publicly accessible areas.
DPIAs must include:
- Description of processing and purposes.
- Necessity and proportionality assessment.
- Risk assessment to data subjects' rights.
- Mitigation measures.

### Data Protection by Design and Default (Article 25)
- **By design**: Integrate data protection into processing systems and operations from the outset.
- **By default**: Process only data necessary for each specific purpose.

### Security of Processing (Article 32)
Must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures:
- **Pseudonymization and encryption**.
- **Confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience** of systems.
- **Ability to restore access** in a timely manner after incidents.
- **Regular testing and evaluation** of security measures.

### Codes of Conduct and Certification (Articles 40-43)
- Adherence to approved codes of conduct may demonstrate compliance.
- Certification under approved mechanisms (e.g., EDPB seal) may demonstrate compliance.

### Penalties Structure (Article 83)
| Tier | Maximum Fine | Violations |
|------|-------------|------------|
| Lower | EUR 10M or 2% of global annual turnover | Breach notification, records, DPIAs, DPO requirements, certification, monitoring body obligations |
| Upper | EUR 20M or 4% of global annual turnover | Basic principles, consent, data subject rights, international transfers, non-compliance with supervisory authority order |
